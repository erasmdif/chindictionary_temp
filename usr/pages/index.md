        <h2>CHIN-DICTIONARY</h2>
        <div class="ch-home-copy">
          <p>CHIN-DICTIONARY is devoted to Basilio Brollo’s (1648–1704) <em>Dictionarium sinico-latinum</em>, one of the major landmarks in Sino-European lexicography. For more than a century, the dictionary circulated mainly through manuscript copies and was valued as an essential tool for the study of Chinese. At the beginning of the nineteenth century, a reworked version was published in Paris without properly acknowledging the authorship of the original work.</p>
          <p>The project approaches the dictionary not simply as a lexical repertory, but as a historical source documenting linguistic forms, practices of classification, and strategies of mediation between European and Chinese intellectual traditions. Its aim is to transform this documentation into searchable and interoperable data while preserving the structure and material organisation of the original work.</p>
          <p>The research combines lexicography, lexicology, archival research, and methods developed within missionary linguistics. The digital platform makes the text searchable and browsable by loci, characters, readings, definitions, and internal relations, while at the same time offering a reconstruction of the manuscript’s original organisation.</p>
          <p><a href="https://research.uniroma1.it/chin-dictionary-brollos-dictionarium-sinico-latinum-linguistic-innovations-textual-connections-and" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer">Project page on Ricerc@Sapienza ↗</a></p>

        <h2>Basilio Brollo</h2>
        <div class="ch-home-copy">
          <p>Basilio Brollo, also known as Basilio da Gemona, was born in Gemona in 1648. A Franciscan friar, after studying and teaching philosophy and theology he left for Asia in 1680. His journey to China lasted almost four years: after travelling through Siam and surviving a shipwreck near Batavia, he reached Canton in 1684.</p>
          <p>In the following years he carried out missionary work in several provinces and devoted himself intensively to the study of Chinese. In Nanjing he compiled two Chinese–Latin dictionaries: a first version, dated 1694 and arranged by radicals and strokes, and a larger second version, produced around 1699 and organised according to pronunciation. The two works described approximately seven thousand and nine thousand characters respectively.</p>
          <p>The <em>Dictionarium</em> became a reference work for generations of European scholars. Its manuscript copies circulated for a long time before a reworked version was published in Paris in 1813 by Chrétien-Louis-Joseph de Guignes without adequately acknowledging Brollo. Already in the following year, Abel-Rémusat contributed to restoring the work’s correct attribution.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="ch-home-pullquote">A work born from the encounter between European lexicographic practices and Chinese systems for organising characters, destined to exert an influence far beyond the lifetime of its author.</div>
